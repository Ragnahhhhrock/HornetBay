Faster forward: 16x and 32x, and the Viking finds its voice bb1a177

FEATUREAUDIO

Two dials turned this push, one for your patience and one for your ears:

  1. The warp ladder grows two rungs. Z now climbs 2x · 4x · 8x · 16x · 32x before dropping back to normal. Sixteen and thirty-two are for the long hauls — the inbound leg from the far waypoint, the air wing’s full pattern cycle, waiting on a trap queue. The whole world steps with you: traffic, helicopters, the carrier’s steam, all of it.
  2. The S-3 sings turbofan now. Sharp ears caught it: the Viking is no prop — she runs a pair of TF34 turbofans under those wings. SAWBUCK 700 now gets the airliner voice in spectate (J), the high synthesized whine instead of the prop whirr. The C-2 keeps the whirr (hers are honest turboprops), the A-6 keeps the jet loop, and every other ride is unchanged.
271200Z JUL 26

Spectate with your ears: an engine note for every ride 715f0f9

FEATUREAUDIO

Spectate mode (J) used to be a silent film. Now every aircraft you ride sounds like itself, three voices crossfaded by type:

  1. Jets — the Hornet, Tomcat, Viper, Eagle, Warthog, MiG, Flanker and the air wing’s jets — reuse the recorded Amiga engine loops, pitch following speed.
  2. Airliners get a turbofan whine, synthesized fresh: two detuned high sines over a band of compressor hiss that rises with speed. Ride a Liberty 737 up the approach and it sings exactly like the cheap seats.
  3. Props get a whirr — the E-2, the C-2 and the P-3, plus all four helicopters: twin blade-pass saws beating against each other the way two turboprops do, over low rumble.
  4. Ships, the carrier and missiles stay quiet — some rides have no engine note to give.
  5. Notifications come with you. Important messages — radio calls, trap calls, warnings — now print on-screen under the spectate title instead of staying home in the cockpit bar. They ride on-screen in the cockpit view too, no longer confined to the little strip in the panel.
261200Z JUL 26

Weather: clouds, storms and lightning 8c11d77

FEATUREGRAPHICSAUDIO

The R toggle just grew a full weather ladder. Where it once flipped CLEAR/RAIN it now steps through five states — MISSION DEFAULT → CLEAR → CLOUDS → RAIN → STORM — on the main menu, on the plane-select map, and saved between visits:

  1. CLOUDS breaks the original’s cloudless sky — the scattered sprite deck that ships with the sim finally gets to fly, fading in over a couple of seconds with a light dimming of the day. Everything eases; nothing pops.
  2. RAIN implies overcast now — the streak rain you know, under the cloud deck, with the usual gloom dragging the murk in close.
  3. STORM brings the lightning. Every few seconds a jagged channel bolt walks down out of the deck somewhere within a few miles, the whole sky answers with a cold blue-white flash, and the thunder arrives late — delayed by true distance at 343 m/s, a crack decaying into a long low rumble through the audio graph.
  4. Clouds got their own weather channel — separate from the rain blend, so overcast days can exist without a drop of rain, and every state fades smoothly through the same sky/fog/sun gloom the rain always used.
  5. Manners kept: satellite planning maps stay free of rain, murk and bolts; pausing still silences the sky; and missions you authored stay exactly as clear as you left them — MISSION DEFAULT never surprises you.
