Eyes of the fleet: an E-2C overhead, and a wingman on your wing PENDING

The pattern over the bay just got crowded — in the best way. One push brings five new regulars to the airspace, a fix for the Tomcat’s manners, and a voice for the Phoenix:

  1. You no longer launch alone. Every mission sortie now catapults you with an assigned wingman — VIPER TWO — off the deck 2.6 km behind you, joining on your right wing (90 m abeam, 70 m back) as you climb out. He covers a 12 km bubble around you, takes the shot when he has it — real AMRAAMs, real kills — and when a bandit gets on his six he jinks and calls for help: you’ll hear him on the radio and see TWO: DEFENSIVE! flash amber on the HUD.
  2. You can task him. The backquote key (`) opens the wingman orders card: ATTACK MY TARGET, COVER ME, FORM UP, RETURN TO BASE — 1–4 issues the order and he executes to the best of his ability, acknowledging on the radio. (The comma key, our first choice, turned out to be the rudder. He’s a good wingman, but he can’t fly two planes.)
  3. The cruiser’s helicopter actually flies. An SH-60 Seahawk spools up on the guided-missile cruiser’s aft deck, lifts, flies a four-leg circuit around the group, then comes home and lands on a moving deck — the approach math chases the ship itself, steaming at 15 knots. Five new helipads — SFO, Oakland, Moffett, Alameda and the SF Heliport downtown — host shuttle helicopters running pad-to-pad across the bay.
  4. A P-3 Orion works the sea lanes. Twelve kilometres west of the group she flies an 11×5 km oval at 500 feet, MAD boom out, hunting submarines — and every couple of minutes she breaks off to make a visual inspection pass 400 m abeam of a surface contact, announcing each run on the radio.
  5. SCREWTOPS is on station. An E-2C Hawkeye from VAW-123 orbits the bay at angels 23, rotodome turning, and calls the fight the way a real Hawkeye does: BOGEY with clock code, miles and angels when a hostile closes; CIVIL TRAFFIC when an airliner conflicts with your altitude; a FRIENDLIES round-up and a full PICTURE every couple of minutes. Bullseye is Alcatraz — check in and she’ll tell you so herself.
  6. Housekeeping for the Tomcat. Her swing-wings now sweep the right way — aft, hugging the fuselage, not forward like a duck (a rotation-sign bug, caught and fixed) — and the contrail tips follow the gloves as they travel. The Phoenix found its voice too: selecting AIM-54 now gets the same spoken callout as Sidewinder, AMRAAM and guns. All three new birds are in the gallery, which now hangs fourteen aircraft.

  1. She’s fully carrier-qualified. Cat 1 holdback and stroke off the bow, tailhook and wires back aboard — the same boat ops as the Hornet, with the Tomcat’s heavier, thirstier twin-engine personality (and a higher ceiling: those TF30s breathe up to 50,000 ft).

  3. Four AIM-54 Phoenix in the tunnel, two Sidewinders on the glove pylons. The Phoenix is a different class of weapon from anything else in the bay: fatter, faster, and fired from far beyond the horizon — radar-guided all the way, FOX 3. It rides the rails from 1.2 out to 90 kilometres.

Spectate: the numpad joins the camera crew a1374c9

The J spectate camera now answers to the numeric keypad — the same keys the original used for its external view, so muscle memory transfers:

  1. KP 4 / 6 pan around the aircraft or hull you’re riding, KP 8 / 2 tilt the camera up and down — the exact motions SHIFT+ARROWS and right-drag already did.

  5. The manual and the on-screen hint under the SPECTATE banner both list the new keys.

Weather: clouds, storms and lightning 8c11d77

The R toggle just grew a full weather ladder. Where it once flipped CLEAR/RAIN it now steps through five states — MISSION DEFAULT → CLEAR → CLOUDS → RAIN → STORM — on the main menu, on the plane-select map, and saved between visits:

  1. CLOUDS breaks the original’s cloudless sky — the scattered sprite deck that ships with the sim finally gets to fly, fading in over a couple of seconds with a light dimming of the day. Everything eases; nothing pops.
  2. RAIN implies overcast now — the streak rain you know, under the cloud deck, with the usual gloom dragging the murk in close.
  3. STORM brings the lightning. Every few seconds a jagged channel bolt walks down out of the deck somewhere within a few miles, the whole sky answers with a cold blue-white flash, and the thunder arrives late — delayed by true distance at 343 m/s, a crack decaying into a long low rumble through the audio graph.
  4. Clouds got their own weather channel — separate from the rain blend, so overcast days can exist without a drop of rain, and every state fades smoothly through the same sky/fog/sun gloom the rain always used.
  5. Manners kept: satellite planning maps stay free of rain, murk and bolts; pausing still silences the sky; and missions you authored stay exactly as clear as you left them — MISSION DEFAULT never surprises you.

Meet the fleet: ALLIED AIRLINES 747-400 6ef1315

The flag carrier of the Bay. ALLIED’s 747-400s run the trans-Pacific trunk — Tokyo, Hong Kong, Sydney — every departure threading the Golden Gate on climb-out, every arrival letting down over the East Bay for SFO’s 28s. 388 souls aboard, four big fans at a rumble you can feel from your own cockpit.
