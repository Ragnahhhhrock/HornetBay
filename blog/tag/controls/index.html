Mission spectate — K rides the cast of the sortie, and nobody else 9410915

J has always been the grand tour: every contact in the bay, airliners and all, nearest first. Sometimes you don’t want the grand tour — you want to check on the fight you’re actually in. K is the new ring: the same ride-along camera, the same pan-and-zoom controls, but it only cycles the cast of the mission you’re flying.

  • The ring assembles itself. Whatever the brief sends up — the Bear pack and their Flanker shotgun, Screwtops 601 holding her orbit — plus VIPER TWO, always. K steps forward through the cast, SHIFT+K steps back, and your own cockpit waits at the seam, same as J.
  • Launched weapons board the ring. When the Bear lets her Kitchens go, the cruise missiles join the cycle as they leave the rails — ride one all the way in and watch what the Phalanx guns are up against.
  • The bay stays out of it. Airliners on approach, the deck cycle, the Orion on her oval — ambient life keeps flying, but it doesn’t take your K presses. J still has the whole scope when you want everyone.

The HUD banner reads MISSION SPECTATE so you always know which ring you’re on. In free flight there is no cast — K says so and points you back to J. — HB

Time acceleration tops out at 16x — and SHIFT+Z walks you back down the ladder c47cbb6

A small quality-of-life tune for the fast-forward crowd. The Z key still cycles time acceleration up through 2x · 4x · 8x · 16x and back to normal — but the 32x rung is gone. Past 16x the sim was stepping the world so many times per frame that a long transit ran hot for no real gain in getting-there.

  • Both directions on one key. SHIFT+Z steps back down the ring — 16x > 8x > 4x > 2x — so if you overshoot the rung you wanted you no longer have to lap the whole ladder to get back to it. Same convention as the spectate key: J rides forward, SHIFT+J rides back.
  • No fights with the burner. SHIFT is a hold-to-burn control, not a tap, so SHIFT+Z borrows it cleanly — same as SHIFT+J and SHIFT+E already do.

16x gets you across the bay in a hurry. 32x just got you past the checkpoint you meant to watch. — HB

Every rung of the ladder: the great consistency pass 697ca69

A full audit of every menu, sub-menu and page on the site — hunting for dead ends, clipped text, hidden keys and wandering navigation. Nine issues found, nine issues fixed:

In the cockpit

  1. ESC was dead on the free-flight map. Pick FREE FLIGHT, change your mind — and there was no way back short of reloading. ESC now steps back to the menu.
  2. ESC was dead on plane select. Same trap, one screen later. ESC now steps back one level: to the start-point map for free flight, to the menu for a mission (where BACK TO MISSION waits).
  3. The plane-select line was clipped. Five jets and the toggles on one line ran clean off both edges of the screen — you literally could not see the F-16 or the Tomcat. The bar is now a three-row panel: two rows of aircraft, then TIME / WX / ESC, every option visible.
  4. Every selection bar now says so: ESC — BACK rides the map select, plane select and briefing bars.
  5. Main-menu keys renumbered and shown. The rows were bound to 1, 2, 6, 8, 9 — with the badges hidden, so three dead keys and no way to discover the live ones. Now 1–5, sequential, and every row wears its key like the sub-menus always did.
  6. Mission keys every keyboard has. The board was bound F1–F16 — and F13–F16 don't exist on most keyboards, leaving four missions mouse-only. The board now runs 1–9, 0, A–F with badges shown; F1–F12 stay bound as silent aliases for anyone who learned them.
  7. FLIGHT MANUAL wears its H.

On the site

  1. One site ring on every page. Cross-links used to wander the headers — STORE · ASSETS here, STORE · ASSETS · PRINT there, nothing at all on the blog. Now all 209 pages carry the same row under the nav buttons: SQUADRON STORE · WALLPAPERS & ASSETS · 3D PRINT BAY · ANALYTICS, with the current section painted amber. It lives in the shared template, so every future page is born with it.
  2. Sub-headings in posts are styled — amber, letter-spaced, on-guide — instead of falling back to browser defaults.

What survived the audit unchanged: the masthead stack, the two-button nav rail, the blurb sizes, the search rail, the tag cloud and the mobile stacking — all already in formation. One look, one feel, every page.

241200Z JUL 26

Q toggles the menu now: quit out, Q back in a083612

BUG FIXCONTROLS

A pilot reported the trap: mid-mission you hit Q to call up the main menu — and there was no way back. The menu slammed down over the cockpit and the only way out was to abandon the sortie and relaunch. The sortie itself was never the problem — your jet waits exactly where you left it while the attract demo borrows the camera — the menu just never offered the way back.

Now it does, two ways. Quit to the menu with Q and you’ll find a new top entry: Q — RESUME FLIGHT, tagged BACK IN THE COCKPIT. Hit Q again (or click it) and the menu folds away mid-frame, mission intact, hands back on the stick. The flag clears the moment you launch a fresh sortie, so there’s no ghost resume waiting after you’ve moved on. The subtle part of the fix: the resume key is polled in the frame loop rather than fired from the key event itself — otherwise the same keypress gets seen by both states and bounces you straight back out to the menu. Fifty-one checks green, and the Bay forgives an accidental Q.

231200Z JUL 26

First solo: a quick-start card, a DEMO that demos, and a trackpad stick 9a4a5df

CONTROLSUX

First feedback from a new pilot on a MacBook: "can't get off the ground." Fair — the sim never told you how. Three fixes. Every launch now opens a quick-start card with the handful of keys that get you airborne (W to firewall the throttles, B brakes off, pull back at 150 knots, G gear up, ? for the full manual) — it fades by itself, closes on a click, and has a don't-show-again tick for veterans. The DEMO menu item actually demos now: it hides the menu and plays the attract loop full-screen until any key brings you back (previously it did exactly nothing, since the demo jet was already flying behind the menu). And the mouse stick grows up: press Y, then click the screen to capture the cursor — pointer lock turns relative movement into stick deflection, which is how the Amiga original flew and, crucially, the only way a trackpad can.
