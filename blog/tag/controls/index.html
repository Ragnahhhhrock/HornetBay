Spectate: the numpad joins the camera crew a1374c9

The J spectate camera now answers to the numeric keypad — the same keys the original used for its external view, so muscle memory transfers:

  1. KP 4 / 6 pan around the aircraft or hull you’re riding, KP 8 / 2 tilt the camera up and down — the exact motions SHIFT+ARROWS and right-drag already did.
  2. KP + / − zoom in and out (KP 9 / 3 work too, matching the original’s POV keys), joining the wheel and [ ].
  3. KP 0 reframes — snap back to the default trailing shot, same as the top-row 0.
  4. No friendly fire: outside spectate the keypad still drives your own jet’s external orbit view exactly as before — the two camera crews never fight over the keys, and leaving spectate never surprises you with a hijacked viewpoint.
  5. The manual and the on-screen hint under the SPECTATE banner both list the new keys.
2026-07-25FEATURECONTROLS

Ride along: spectate every aircraft in the sky 15cd3e7

Ever wondered what the sky looks like from a cable’s length behind a 747? Press J in flight and find out: you hop out of your own cockpit and ride along with the next aircraft in the area — airliners on their routes, MiGs on patrol, tankers, wingmen, the whole cast. Keep tapping J to cycle through everyone airborne; after the last contact you’re handed back to your own cockpit, and V or X brings you home instantly at any point. If your ride gets splashed or leaves the area, the camera lets go and you’re back in your own seat — no falling out of the sky required.

Press U to make the HUD vanish entirely — clean screenshots, clean footage, just you and the view — and again to bring every gauge back. And the fuel trucks have been generous one more time: burn is halved again, with the Hornet sipping 20 at military power and 47.5 in burner (the Falcon 18.75/43.75), so the long haul out to the fleet no longer ends as a glider lesson. Both keys are written up in the in-game manual (?), as regulation requires.

2026-07-25FEATURECONTROLS

X marks the cockpit, and a fistful of quality-of-life 275a571

A pilot request, granted on the spot: X snaps you straight back to the cockpit from any camera — chase, orbit, tower — without cycling the whole carousel with V. The two keys sit side by side for a reason: V wanders out, X comes home. The flight manual says so, in writing.

Radar range now steps the way a pilot actually fights: it starts wide at 40 miles and each tap of R tightens the noose — 40, 10, 2, and back around to 40. Fuel burn has been halved again, roughly doubling your legs: loiter far, chase long, and still make the deck with something in the tanks. And when it all goes wrong — crash or eject — there’s no paperwork anymore: the wreck settles, your best score is banked, and you’re back at the menu ready to launch the next one.

Also aboard: a console chirp when you pause or call up the menu, so the bay answers when you ask it to hold — and the aircraft gallery swells to eight, with all four airliners in their house liveries, so you can get a proper look at the traffic before you try very hard not to shoot it.

2026-07-24BUGFIXCONTROLS

Q toggles the menu now: quit out, Q back in a083612

A pilot reported the trap: mid-mission you hit Q to call up the main menu — and there was no way back. The menu slammed down over the cockpit and the only way out was to abandon the sortie and relaunch. The sortie itself was never the problem — your jet waits exactly where you left it while the attract demo borrows the camera — the menu just never offered the way back.

Now it does, two ways. Quit to the menu with Q and you’ll find a new top entry: Q — RESUME FLIGHT, tagged BACK IN THE COCKPIT. Hit Q again (or click it) and the menu folds away mid-frame, mission intact, hands back on the stick. The flag clears the moment you launch a fresh sortie, so there’s no ghost resume waiting after you’ve moved on. The subtle part of the fix: the resume key is polled in the frame loop rather than fired from the key event itself — otherwise the same keypress gets seen by both states and bounces you straight back out to the menu. Fifty-one checks green, and the Bay forgives an accidental Q.

2026-07-23CONTROLSUX

First solo: a quick-start card, a DEMO that demos, and a trackpad stick 9a4a5df

First feedback from a new pilot on a MacBook: "can't get off the ground." Fair — the sim never told you how. Three fixes. Every launch now opens a quick-start card with the handful of keys that get you airborne (W to firewall the throttles, B brakes off, pull back at 150 knots, G gear up, ? for the full manual) — it fades by itself, closes on a click, and has a don't-show-again tick for veterans. The DEMO menu item actually demos now: it hides the menu and plays the attract loop full-screen until any key brings you back (previously it did exactly nothing, since the demo jet was already flying behind the menu). And the mouse stick grows up: press Y, then click the screen to capture the cursor — pointer lock turns relative movement into stick deflection, which is how the Amiga original flew and, crucially, the only way a trackpad can.

2026-07-22BUG FIXCONTROLS

The gun answers SPACE and ENTER — with tracers you can see d0ee2a7

Three fixes in one pass. The gun simply ignored SPACE — only the mouse trigger worked, because the fire code never looked at the keyboard for the Vulcan. Now SPACE and ENTER both fire the selected weapon (hold either for a burst), so weapon select moves to TAB, with 1 / 2 / 3 jumping straight to AMRAAM, Sidewinder or gun. And the tracers themselves are rebuilt: the old bolts were pencil-thin and effectively invisible from the cockpit, so each round now flies as a fat glowing bolt that reads day or night.
