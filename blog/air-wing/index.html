Around the clock: the Big E works her 1994 air wing 86ef63b

The flight deck is no longer a quiet place to be left alone with your thoughts. The Enterprise now works her 1994 Pacific air wing around the clock — three period squadrons flying a continuous launch–pattern–trap–park cycle whether you are watching or not:

  1. KNIGHT 501 — the VA-52 A-6E Intruder — SAWBUCK 700, the VS-37 S-3B Viking, and PROVIDER 30, the VRC-30 C-2 Greyhound, park on the starboard row, taxi the safe lane to Cat 2 (Cat 1 stays yours), and get flung off the bow in turn.
  2. They fly the pattern like professionals: climb off the bow, the break, a port-side downwind at 850 feet, base, and the final gate — then down the 3.5° groove to the angle deck and the 3-wire. Rollout, taxi clear, park, and go again.
  3. The radio knows the script: CAT 2 — YOU’RE AWAY on the shot, BOLTER, BOLTER if the wires are missed (they go around, honestly), and TRAPPED ABOARD when the hook takes. The feed calls the 3-wire by name.
  4. Discipline, even ambient: the air wing holds a lap if you are on final, and only one jet works the groove at a time — the rest stack politely in the pattern. Taxi routes respect your spot on Cat 1.
  5. Everywhere a friendly should be: blue contacts on radar with their callsigns, named on the tactical map (N), and spectate-able with J — ride the COD down the groove from the LSO’s seat sometime.
  6. Why 1994 matters: all three squadrons were the real Pacific operators that summer — VA-52 and VS-37 in CVW-15, VRC-30 Det 1 flying its first cruise — researched, liveried, and parked on the wrong carrier entirely, as tradition allows. Missions involving these airframes come later; the flightpaths are already here.
